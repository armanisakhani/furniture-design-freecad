# Furniture Design — Domain Glossary

Domain glossary for this repo's FreeCAD parametric furniture designs. Geometry and fabrication logic live in code; this file only defines the vocabulary. **Shared** terms below apply to every design in `furniture/`; each design then gets its own section for its own vocabulary.

## Shared — Fabrication core

**Visible panel / stock source**:
Every panel carries a `visible` (bool) and `stock_source` ("new" / "reclaimed") attribute. Panels not visible in the finished, assembled piece are cut from reclaimed MDF the user already owns, to save cost without affecting appearance; panels visible in the finished piece are cut from new stock. Assignment is a judgment call based on the panel's role in the assembly (e.g. drawer backs/bottoms and internal dividers are typically hidden), not a fixed rule. Each furniture module supplies its own reclaimed/new colors from its own `params.py` (see `core/panel.py`'s `create_assembly_panel`).
_Avoid_: treating this as the same distinction as the bed project's reference spreadsheet's old/new columns — that was an unrelated, discarded attempt to shrink dimensions to fit the mattress, not a stock-sourcing decision.

**MDF thickness**:
The core board's own face-to-face thickness. Drives box and drawer fitting geometry; each furniture module defines its own confirmed value in its own `params.py`.
_Avoid_: "panel thickness" as a synonym — that name is retired; it used to conflate the board core with the edge-banding finish.

**PVC edge banding**:
A thin tape glued onto exposed cut edges to hide the raw MDF core. Wraps the edge only — it does not add to MDF thickness or affect fitting geometry, only the visible edge profile and later the cut list. Which panel edges get it is a per-panel decision, made when panels are defined. Not modeled as its own panel object (no PVC-material geometry exists yet, despite `Panel.Material` supporting a `"PVC"` value for future use) — where a banded edge needs to actually render a color, it's done as a plain `PanelColor` override on the bearing panel instead (e.g. `furniture/bed/params.py`'s `DRAWER_OPENING_EDGE_MATCHES_BODY` / `box.py`'s `edge_color`, for the Box shell edges that become visible around the drawer opening in `DRAWER_STYLE="inset"`).
_Avoid_: assuming it changes overall panel dimensions the way MDF thickness does.

## Bed / Drawer-Box System (`furniture/bed/`)

**Box** (also "bed box"):
One of `BOX_COUNT` (3) identical shared shell units placed side by side that together form the full bed. Each Box is one shell (top, bottom, 2 long side walls, 1 center divider) containing 2 Drawer_boxes that open from opposite faces.
_Avoid_: "drawer box" for this — that term now means something narrower (see below).

**Drawer_box**:
One individual pull-out drawer unit that sits inside a Box. `DRAWERS_PER_BOX` (2) live in each Box, opening from opposite faces, for `BOX_COUNT * DRAWERS_PER_BOX` (6) total across the bed.
_Avoid_: "drawer" alone (ambiguous with Box in conversation), "drawer box" as a synonym for Box.

**Skirt** (apron):
A thin decorative MDF trim board hanging down from the underside of a Box, near the floor. Unrelated to drawer position — the drawer lives up near the top of the Box; the skirt is a separate board down near its base. With a leg frame (`HAS_LEG_FRAME`, `params.py` — see Support frame / Assembly variants), its height covers only part of the leg frame's height; the remaining gap below it stays open as hand-clearance space for reaching under the handle-less drawer front. With `HAS_LEG_FRAME=False` (only valid for `DRAWER_STYLE="inset"`, e.g. `STYLE=5`) there's no floor gap left to bridge toward, so the end skirt's own reach collapses to nothing — `EndFaceFoot` (`bed.py`) stops exactly at the Box's own bottom (Z=0), the actual floor in that case, instead of dangling below it. Comes in two independently toggled kinds: the drawer-side skirt (on the two long, drawer-carrying faces) and the end skirt (on the two short head/foot faces, which carry no drawer — optional, purely for visual continuity, still a real separate panel in Phase 6).
_Correction_: the drawer-side skirt is **not** a separate panel in the 2 `"overlay_*"` `DRAWER_STYLE`s (Model A / `"overlay_under_box"`) — each Drawer_box's own Face panel already extends down `SKIRT_HEIGHT` below the box's bottom (see Face below), doing that job. In `"inset"` (Model B, push-to-open) there is no drawer-side skirt at all — the Face doesn't protrude or reach down, so that space is simply left open (no hand-clearance need either, since push-to-open needs no leg-frame finger gap). Only the end skirt is a real, separately built panel in all styles.

**Structural front** vs **Face** (نما):
A Drawer_box's front is 2 separate panels, not one: the structural front is part of the carcass (same size/role as Back, mirrored), hidden once assembled; the Face is a separate panel screwed onto the structural front's outer face — the one that's actually visible. The Face always mounts flush against the structural front and protrudes outward by its own thickness (`DRAWER_FRONT_OVERLAY_AMOUNT`); what differs by `DRAWER_STYLE` (see Assembly variants below) is where the structural front itself sits (`DRAWER_FRONT_SETBACK`) and how big the Face itself is:
  * `"overlay_over_box"` (Model A) / `"overlay_under_box"`: setback 0 — the structural front sits flush with the Box's own open face, so the Face's protrusion reaches all the way out to `FRAME_WIDTH`. The Face is also much taller than the drawer opening (top edge aligned with the drawer's own top, bottom edge extending `SKIRT_HEIGHT` below the Box's own bottom, see Skirt above) and narrower than the Box's own Y-footprint by `DRAWER_FACE_GAP` (a reveal against the neighboring Box's Face).
  * `"inset"` (Model B, push-to-open): setback equals `DRAWER_FRONT_OVERLAY_AMOUNT` — the structural front (and the whole Drawer_box carcass behind it, including where its internal transverse wall sits) recedes into the Box by exactly the Face's own thickness, so the Face's protrusion is fully absorbed and lands flush with the Box's own open face instead of past it. No handle: opened by pushing the front, which needs the front recessed relative to the frame rather than overlaying it. No skirt/top-cap duty. The Face is sized to the Box's actual interior opening (`BOX_INTERIOR_HEIGHT` tall, between the 2 side walls wide, minus `DRAWER_FACE_OPENING_GAP`) rather than the (narrower) drawer carcass behind it — a real push-to-open front's only job is covering the hole in the Box's face, unrelated to how wide the mechanism behind it happens to be. Also flags `DRAWER_OPENING_EDGE_MATCHES_BODY` (see PVC edge banding entry below) since Bottom/the 2 side walls now show a thin sliver of their own front edge around the Face.
_Avoid_: treating "drawer front" as a single panel. Also avoid assuming the Face's protrusion amount changes between styles — it's always `DRAWER_FRONT_OVERLAY_AMOUNT`; only the structural front's setback and the Face's own Y/Z size change.

**Internal transverse wall** (دیواره عرضی داخلی, "پشت کشو" = behind the drawer):
A purely structural MDF wall inside a Box, positioned `RAIL_BACK_CLEARANCE` behind a Drawer_box's back — not part of the drawer, not touching it. Its job is to keep the Box's top panel from sagging over the span between the box's two long side walls. Each Box has 2 of them, one behind each of its 2 drawers; since drawer depth (`RAIL_LENGTH`) is much less than half of `FRAME_WIDTH`, the 2 walls don't meet in the middle — there's real open, unused box interior between them (confirmed against `bed-1-box.jpg`, which shows hardware sitting loose in that gap, and the reference cut-list spreadsheet).
_Avoid_: "center divider" — assuming the gap between the 2 walls is itself a modeled part (it's just open, unmodeled space).

**Box shell inset / `DRAWER_STYLE`**:
Bottom, the 2 long side walls, and the drawer carcasses are always inset from `FRAME_WIDTH` by `MDF_THICKNESS` on each X side — this is `BOX_WIDTH`. They don't bear the mattress, so this inset is harmless on its own — but *something* always has to reach back out to exactly `FRAME_WIDTH`, or the mattress silently overhangs unsupported. Which thing does that reaching depends on `DRAWER_STYLE` (`furniture/bed/params.py`'s `_drawer_style_geometry()`, the single place this is decided):
  * `"overlay_over_box"`: the Top panel itself extends all the way to `FRAME_WIDTH` and caps the drawer's Face from above, instead of a separate top-trim part (the reference photos actually show a separate applied piece doing this job — a deliberate deviation from the reference, at the user's explicit request).
  * `"overlay_under_box"`: the Top panel stays inset like the rest of the shell (an unmodeled rail-mount frame sits above the drawer and reaches out instead, so the drawer carcass loses one `MDF_THICKNESS` of height for it) — but the Face bridges the gap instead, since it spans nearly the Box's *entire* Y-length (`box_length - DRAWER_FACE_GAP`), not just the drawer's own narrow width.
  * `"inset"`: the Face deliberately doesn't protrude at all (that's the point), so it was a real bug for the Top panel to also stay inset here — nothing bridged the gap, so the mattress overhung `MDF_THICKNESS` unsupported the bed's entire length, invisible only because `MATTRESS_TO_FRAME_GAP_WIDTH > 0` already tucked the mattress in short of it (caught once `STYLE=4` set that gap to 0). Fixed: the Top panel reaches `FRAME_WIDTH` here too, same as `"overlay_over_box"` — independent of `drawer_height_reduction` (still applies, an unrelated rail-hardware-clearance concern) and of the front staying recessed.
_Avoid_: "top trim" as a separate part — retired. Also avoid assuming the Box shell sits flush at `FRAME_WIDTH` — only the Top panel does (in `"overlay_over_box"`/`"inset"`), or the Face does (in `"overlay_under_box"`) — Bottom and the 2 side walls never do.

**Geometry convention**:
**Length** is the head-to-toe direction of the bed (mattress dimension 2000 mm). **Width** is the side-by-side direction (mattress dimension 1800 mm).

**Assembly variants**:
One parameter, `DRAWER_STYLE` (`params.py`), covers how the drawer meets the Box — collapsed from 2 separate, independently-settable knobs (`DRAWER_OVERLAY_STYLE` + `DRAWER_FRONT_MODE`) once it became clear only 3 of their 2x2 combinations are real design intents:
  * `"overlay_over_box"` (**Model A**, `STYLE=1`, the default): no handle, opened by hand from underneath; requires a leg/support frame to raise the bed for finger clearance under the drawer fronts. The front (its Face) protrudes to `FRAME_WIDTH`, and the Box's own Top panel also reaches out to `FRAME_WIDTH`, capping the Face from above.
  * `"overlay_under_box"` (`STYLE=2`): front also protrudes to `FRAME_WIDTH`, but the Top panel instead stays inset with the rest of the shell — an unmodeled rail-mount frame sits above the drawer and reaches out to meet the Face instead ("under": the drawer sits under the Box's own top plane, not capped by it).
  * `"inset"` (**Model B**, push-to-open, `STYLE=3`/`STYLE=4`/`STYLE=5`): no leg frame needed, drawer front sits inset (flush with the Box's own open face, not protruding past it, sized to the Box's actual interior opening — see Structural front/Face above). Whether the leg frame is actually dropped is its own toggle, `HAS_LEG_FRAME` (only settable `False` here — see Skirt below and Support frame section of `params.py`); `STYLE=5` is the only preset that does.
Select with the `STYLE` env var (`params.py`'s `STYLES` dict); `DRAWER_STYLE`/`MATTRESS_TO_FRAME_GAP_WIDTH` can each still be overridden individually on top of a selected style via their own same-named env var.
